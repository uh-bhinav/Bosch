Yes. I would attach a **normative ideal-pipeline specification** to the forensic prompt. The key is that Claude should not interpret this as "suggestions." It should treat this as the **target mathematical/engineering behavior** and measure the repository against it.

One correction to the earlier framing: **do not define the ideal system as "bad draft → undercut."** That is exactly the conceptual mistake your current output appears to be making. Draft quality and withdrawal obstruction are separate analyses. The pull-direction optimizer may use both, but one must never substitute for the other.

Use the following as the plan/specification attached after the main forensic prompt.

```text
REFERENCE TARGET: IDEAL PULL-DIRECTION + UNDERCUT ANALYSIS PIPELINE

This section defines the intended behavior of the system. Treat it as the normative target architecture against which the current implementation must be measured. Do not reinterpret it into a different algorithm. The purpose is to determine deviations between the repository and this target, then propose the minimum technically correct changes required to reach this behavior.

1. FUNDAMENTAL GEOMETRIC MODEL

Represent every candidate pull direction as a normalized 3D unit vector:

    d = (dx, dy, dz)
    ||d|| = 1

d is the direction in which the molded part is withdrawn relative to the mold.

The system must support arbitrary normalized directions. The six principal directions:

    +X, -X, +Y, -Y, +Z, -Z

are preferred initial candidates, not hard restrictions.

Additional diagonal and arbitrary spherical directions may be evaluated when necessary. The search must therefore be hierarchical rather than restricted to the coordinate axes.

The opposite direction -d represents the withdrawal direction of the opposite mold half. The system must reason about both sides of the mold, not assume that every surface must be released in +d.

2. DRAFT ANALYSIS IS A SEPARATE SIGNAL

For every face and candidate direction d, calculate the directional relationship between the face normal n and d.

The fundamental quantity is:

    c = n · d

and, where useful:

    theta = acos(clamp(c, -1, 1))

The exact convention for cavity/core side and draft angle must remain consistent throughout the pipeline.

Draft answers:

    "How favorably is this surface oriented relative to the proposed withdrawal direction?"

It does NOT answer:

    "Is this surface geometrically trapped?"

A face can have:
- excellent draft and still participate in an undercut;
- poor/zero draft and still be completely withdrawable;
- zero draft because it is a normal vertical wall;
- a negative directional normal relationship because it belongs to the opposite mold side.

Therefore:

    BAD_DRAFT != UNDERCUT

and:

    CORE/CAVITY_SIDE != UNDERCUT

and:

    CONCAVITY != UNDERCUT

Draft must never be used as the final definition of an undercut.

3. WHAT A TRUE UNDERCUT MEANS

For a proposed pull direction d, an undercut is a region of geometry that cannot be withdrawn from its corresponding mold half along its permitted withdrawal direction without intersecting retained part geometry.

The defining property is therefore:

    GEOMETRIC WITHDRAWAL OBSTRUCTION

not:

    poor draft angle.

For a surface region intended to be released in direction r, where r is either +d or -d, consider the open withdrawal ray from points on that surface:

    p(t) = p + t r, t > 0

A surface region is directionally accessible if withdrawal along r does not encounter another portion of the part that blocks the motion.

If another portion of the solid lies in the withdrawal path and produces a genuine positive-area obstruction, that region is a candidate undercut.

The system must distinguish:

    accessible
    obstructed
    ambiguous/unknown

rather than collapsing all non-ideal conditions into "undercut."

4. ACCESSIBILITY IS THE PRIMARY GEOMETRIC TEST

Accessibility is the bridge between directional analysis and actual undercut detection.

For each candidate pull direction d:

    evaluate accessibility from +d and -d

for relevant surface regions.

The conceptual test is:

    Can this surface region move away from the part along its assigned mold-half withdrawal direction without colliding with the remaining solid?

This can be implemented using appropriate OCC geometric operations such as ray/visibility tests, swept volumes, interference tests, or equivalent exact/robust geometric reasoning.

The implementation mechanism may differ, but the semantic result must be equivalent to physical withdrawal.

The test must not merely ask:

    Is n · d below a threshold?

It must ask:

    Does geometry actually block translation along the withdrawal direction?

5. LOCAL SURFACE ORIENTATION VS GLOBAL OBSTRUCTION

The algorithm must evaluate both local and global geometry.

Local information:

    face normal
    draft angle
    face orientation
    curvature/adjacency
    cavity/core relationship

Global information:

    other solid geometry lying along the withdrawal path
    re-entrant geometry
    enclosed pockets
    lips/hooks
    opposing walls
    channels
    ribs
    bosses
    geometric occlusion

Local orientation is useful for inexpensive filtering and scoring.

Global obstruction determines whether an actual undercut exists.

Therefore a heuristic may identify:

    POSSIBLE_UNDERCUT

but only geometric validation may establish:

    CONFIRMED_UNDERCUT

6. PROXY UNDERCUTS

Cheap heuristics are permitted and desirable for performance, but they are only candidate generators.

Examples of useful evidence may include:

    unfavorable directional orientation
    concave/re-entrant topology
    local geometric relationships
    accessibility-risk indicators
    face adjacency
    curvature
    projected geometry

These may produce:

    proxy/suspected undercut candidates

They must never automatically become final undercuts.

The conceptual pipeline is:

    CHEAP EVIDENCE
        ↓
    POSSIBLE CANDIDATE
        ↓
    GEOMETRIC ACCESSIBILITY ANALYSIS
        ↓
    BOOLEAN/EXACT VALIDATION WHEN REQUIRED
        ↓
    CONFIRMED UNDERCUT

7. CONCAVE GEOMETRY

Concavity is evidence, not proof.

A concave edge or re-entrant region may indicate a possible withdrawal obstruction.

But a concave feature can still be accessible along a particular direction.

Therefore:

    concavity → candidate

not:

    concavity → confirmed undercut

The same applies to any current "core-side + concave-edge" heuristic.

Such a heuristic may remain as a cheap prefilter if useful, but it cannot define the final undercut population.

8. BOOLEAN VALIDATION

Expensive OCC Boolean operations should be used to validate geometrically meaningful candidates.

For a candidate surface/feature and withdrawal direction r:

    construct the appropriate swept/translated representation of the geometry that would occupy the withdrawal path.

Then determine whether the swept geometry intersects retained part geometry in a way representing genuine interference.

The exact implementation may use:
- swept solids
- prism extrusion
- translation
- intersection
- common/boolean operations
- collision testing

but the geometric meaning must remain:

    "Would this feature physically interfere with withdrawal along r?"

The implementation must not confuse:
- numerical intersection caused by tolerance,
- coincident/tangent geometry,
- self-intersection,
- the originating face itself,
- local adjacency,

with genuine withdrawal obstruction.

Boolean tolerance and fuzzy tolerance must therefore be handled deliberately.

9. BOOLEAN RESULT SEMANTICS

Boolean processing must produce semantically distinct outcomes.

At minimum:

    CONFIRMED_UNDERCUT
    NOT_UNDERCUT
    UNKNOWN / UNVALIDATED

If the implementation additionally needs:

    BOOLEAN_FAILED
    BOOLEAN_SKIPPED

those are diagnostic states, not automatically geometric states.

Critically:

    BOOLEAN_FAILED != UNDERCUT

and:

    BOOLEAN_SKIPPED != UNDERCUT

A failed or skipped validation may mean:

    "we do not know"

not:

    "the feature is definitely trapped."

The final `undercut_face_ids` or equivalent output must contain only geometry that satisfies the defined confirmation criterion.

Suspected/proxy candidates should remain separately identifiable.

10. ZERO-DRAFT WALLS

A fundamental regression case is a conventional vertical wall.

For example, with:

    d = +Z

a cylindrical or planar vertical wall may have approximately:

    n · d = 0

This means approximately zero draft.

It does NOT mean the wall is an undercut.

If the wall has a clear withdrawal path, it must be classified as:

    zero/low draft
    but accessible
    therefore not an undercut

This distinction is mandatory.

11. GENUINE RE-ENTRANT UNDERCUT

A genuine undercut must demonstrate the opposite behavior.

For a proposed withdrawal direction r:

    surface/feature
        ↓
    withdrawal path
        ↓
    another retained portion of the solid blocks translation

If removing the mold half in direction r would cause geometric collision with the retained part, the feature is an undercut for that mold half.

This should be demonstrable through geometric validation.

12. CORE/CAVITY ASSIGNMENT

Core/cavity classification and undercut detection are related but distinct.

For candidate direction d:

    determine which surfaces are naturally associated with +d
    determine which surfaces are naturally associated with -d

using the chosen normal/directional convention.

This classification helps determine which mold half owns/release-checks a surface.

But:

    mold-side assignment does not prove release obstruction.

A surface can belong to the cavity/core side and still be accessible.

13. PULL-DIRECTION EVALUATION

For every candidate direction d, compute a complete directional analysis.

Conceptually:

    candidate direction d
        ↓
    directional face metrics
        ↓
    draft analysis
        ↓
    accessibility analysis
        ↓
    possible undercut candidates
        ↓
    geometric confirmation
        ↓
    confirmed undercuts
        ↓
    side-action requirements
        ↓
    manufacturing score

The direction must be evaluated as a complete physical hypothesis.

Do not choose a direction solely because it minimizes draft error.

Do not choose a direction solely because it minimizes a heuristic undercut count.

14. DIRECTION SEARCH

The search should be hierarchical.

LEVEL 1:
Evaluate:

    +X
    -X
    +Y
    -Y
    +Z
    -Z

These provide the principal manufacturing directions.

LEVEL 2:
If necessary, evaluate strategically generated diagonal directions.

These can include normalized combinations such as:

    (±1, ±1, 0)
    (±1, 0, ±1)
    (0, ±1, ±1)
    (±1, ±1, ±1)

or equivalent geometrically meaningful candidates.

LEVEL 3:
If no sufficiently good solution exists, search arbitrary directions over the sphere.

The arbitrary search must be normalized and must provide controlled coverage.

LEVEL 4:
Perform local refinement around the strongest candidate directions.

The important principle is:

    principal directions first
    → broader candidates only when justified
    → arbitrary directions when necessary
    → local refinement around promising regions

Arbitrary directions are allowed, but they must not cause the optimizer to immediately select a mathematically slightly better but practically undesirable diagonal direction when a principal direction already provides an equivalent or superior manufacturing solution.

15. DIRECTION SCORE

The final score must combine independent manufacturing signals.

Conceptually:

    SCORE(d) =
        draft_quality(d)
        + accessibility_quality(d)
        + confirmed_undercut_penalty(d)
        + side_action_penalty(d)
        + interference_penalty(d)
        + other validated manufacturing penalties
        + optional preference terms

The exact numerical weights are implementation parameters and must be justified by the intended behavior.

However, the semantics are fixed:

    draft measures draft
    accessibility measures accessibility
    confirmed undercuts measure actual withdrawal obstruction

One metric must not silently substitute for another.

16. PRINCIPAL-DIRECTION PREFERENCE

When two directions are effectively equivalent from a manufacturing perspective, prefer the simpler direction.

For example, if:

    +Z

and:

    (0.707, 0, -0.707)

produce essentially equivalent manufacturing outcomes, +Z should normally win because it is simpler, more conventional, and easier to interpret.

An arbitrary direction should win only when it provides a meaningful validated manufacturing improvement.

The optimizer should therefore avoid rewarding tiny numerical improvements enough to override practical direction simplicity.

17. NO "OPTIMIZATION BY ARTIFACT"

The optimizer must not select a direction because the undercut detector is wrong.

This is a critical requirement.

If the undercut detector incorrectly reports:

    8 undercuts

for one direction and:

    1 undercut

for another direction,

the optimizer may select the second direction even though the geometry is actually better in the first direction.

Therefore:

    correctness of undercut detection
        precedes
    correctness of direction optimization.

The direction optimizer cannot be trusted until the undercut/accessibility semantics are correct.

18. PART1 REGRESSION

`Part1.stp` is a mandatory regression case.

Its known expected primary pull direction is:

    +Z

The corrected pipeline must therefore identify +Z as the preferred direction under the defined scoring/tie-breaking rules.

A diagonal direction must not replace +Z merely because of an artifact in the heuristic undercut calculation.

This case specifically tests whether:

    ordinary vertical walls
    draft analysis
    accessibility
    undercut classification
    direction scoring

are correctly separated.

19. PART3 / ARBITRARY-DIRECTION REGRESSION

`Part3.stp` is used to verify that the system is not artificially restricted to principal axes.

If its geometry genuinely favors a non-principal direction, the optimizer must be capable of discovering it.

The system therefore needs both properties:

    principal directions are preferred when adequate
    arbitrary directions are available when geometrically necessary

These are not contradictory.

20. PARTING LINE

The pull direction should be selected before final parting-line reasoning.

The conceptual relationship is:

    candidate pull direction
        ↓
    manufacturability analysis
        ↓
    preferred pull direction
        ↓
    parting-line/core-cavity reasoning
        ↓
    side-action/remaining-undercut analysis

Parting-line analysis must not silently redefine what constitutes an undercut.

21. SIDE ACTIONS

If a confirmed geometric obstruction remains after selecting the best pull direction, classify the affected feature as requiring an additional action/side core where appropriate.

Conceptually:

    confirmed obstruction
        +
    cannot be resolved by primary pull direction
        ↓
    side action candidate

This is different from:

    bad draft
        ↓
    side action

22. PERFORMANCE ARCHITECTURE

The system must remain computationally practical.

Do not perform expensive geometric operations for every:

    face × direction × possible sample

combination unless absolutely necessary.

Use a staged architecture:

    Stage A:
    cheap directional calculations

    Stage B:
    cheap candidate generation

    Stage C:
    accessibility screening

    Stage D:
    expensive geometric validation only for meaningful candidates

    Stage E:
    direction scoring

    Stage F:
    refinement of promising directions

Directional quantities that depend only on:

    face geometry
    candidate direction

should be cached/reused.

The expensive Boolean/solid operations must never be the only mechanism used for every face in every direction.

23. IMPORTANT: HEURISTIC VS TRUTH

Every output in the pipeline must have a defined epistemic status.

For example:

    heuristic candidate
        = possible problem

    accessibility risk
        = geometric evidence of possible obstruction

    Boolean confirmed
        = validated obstruction

    final undercut
        = confirmed obstruction satisfying the final reporting criterion

The system must not expose a heuristic as if it were a geometric truth.

24. IDEAL END-TO-END ALGORITHM

For each candidate direction d:

STEP 1:
Normalize d.

STEP 2:
For every relevant face, calculate directional quantities such as:

    n · d
    draft angle
    directional side
    preliminary accessibility indicators

STEP 3:
Compute draft statistics independently.

STEP 4:
Generate possible undercut/accessibility-risk candidates using inexpensive geometric/topological evidence.

STEP 5:
For each meaningful candidate, determine actual withdrawal accessibility along the appropriate mold-half direction:

    +d or -d

STEP 6:
Where accessibility is ambiguous or where a high-confidence obstruction must be established, perform robust OCC geometric validation.

STEP 7:
Classify each candidate as:

    confirmed obstruction
    accessible/not undercut
    unknown/unvalidated

STEP 8:
Aggregate only confirmed geometric obstructions into the final undercut result.

Keep proxy/suspected/unknown results separate.

STEP 9:
Calculate side-action requirements from confirmed unresolved withdrawal obstructions.

STEP 10:
Calculate the manufacturing score for direction d using:

    draft
    accessibility
    confirmed undercuts
    side actions
    interference/other validated penalties

STEP 11:
Compare d against all other candidate directions.

STEP 12:
Use hierarchical expansion and local refinement when necessary.

STEP 13:
Select the direction with the best validated manufacturing outcome, using practical simplicity/preference as a tie-breaker rather than allowing tiny numerical differences to dominate.

STEP 14:
Only after the pull direction is established, continue into final parting-line and core/cavity reasoning.

25. FINAL SEMANTIC CONTRACT

The corrected system must obey these relationships:

    bad draft
        DOES NOT IMPLY
    undercut

    core/cavity side
        DOES NOT IMPLY
    undercut

    concavity
        DOES NOT IMPLY
    undercut

    proxy candidate
        DOES NOT IMPLY
    confirmed undercut

    Boolean failure
        DOES NOT IMPLY
    undercut

    Boolean skip
        DOES NOT IMPLY
    undercut

Instead:

    undercut
        =
    genuine withdrawal obstruction
        validated by appropriate geometric reasoning

and:

    optimal pull direction
        =
    direction providing the best validated manufacturing outcome
    under the defined scoring and practical-direction rules.

The final implementation must satisfy the following conceptual tests:

B. A concave feature must not automatically become an undercut.

C. A core/cavity-side classification must not automatically become an undercut.

E. Boolean failures/skips must not silently become confirmed undercuts.

G. Accessibility must represent physical withdrawal, not merely normal orientation.

H. Arbitrary pull directions must remain supported.

K. `Part1.stp` must prefer the known +Z solution.

L. `Part3.stp` must remain capable of producing a genuinely non-principal solution when its geometry actually warrants one.

M. Direction optimization must operate on trustworthy undercut/accessibility results rather than artifacts produced by the undercut detector.

N. The final UI/API must distinguish confirmed geometric results from heuristic/unknown results.

27. MOST IMPORTANT ARCHITECTURAL PRINCIPLE

The entire pipeline should be understood as:

    GEOMETRY
        ↓
    DIRECTIONAL ORIENTATION
        ↓
    DRAFT ANALYSIS

    GEOMETRY
        ↓
    WITHDRAWAL ACCESSIBILITY
        ↓
    POSSIBLE OBSTRUCTION
        ↓
    GEOMETRIC VALIDATION
        ↓
    CONFIRMED UNDERCUT

    DRAFT + ACCESSIBILITY + CONFIRMED UNDERCUTS
        ↓

        ↓
```

### The most important thing this prevents

Your current result:

> `(+0.707, 0, -0.707)` → 8 undercuts → 1 major undercut → therefore "best"

may be **internally consistent but geometrically wrong**.

That's the dangerous kind of bug. The optimizer isn't necessarily "bad" by itself. It can be faithfully optimizing **bad evidence**.

The ideal dependency is:

**geometry → accessibility → confirmed undercuts → direction score**

not:

**geometry → draft/heuristic → fake undercuts → direction score**

And that is why I would make **undercut semantics the first thing Claude audits**, before allowing it to touch the direction optimizer.

Also, I would explicitly tell Claude that the `+Z` result for Part1 is a **regression oracle**, not merely an example. If its proposed corrected algorithm cannot explain why the Part1 geometry produces +Z, the plan is not good enough.

One more important point: **don't ask Claude to make the algorithm "match the Bosch answer" by hardcoding +Z for Part1.** That would be garbage engineering. Part1 should emerge as +Z because the geometry and scoring say +Z is the correct manufacturing direction. The known answer is a validation constraint, not an implementation rule.
